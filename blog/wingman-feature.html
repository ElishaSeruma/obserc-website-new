Meet Your Wingman: Obserc's Guardian Feature for Nights Out

When everyone is responsible for your safety, no one really is. Obserc's Wingman assigns one dedicated person as your Night Out guardian, and that focus makes all the difference.

Back to Blog

There is a well-documented phenomenon in psychology called the bystander effect: when responsibility is diffused across a group, individuals assume someone else will act. Safety tools that alert a whole group suffer from exactly this problem. Everybody receives the alert. Nobody feels personally responsible. Obserc's Wingman feature solves this with a design that is deliberately singular, one person, one responsibility, one relationship.

The Wingman is the person you designate at the start of a Night Out as your personal guardian for the evening. They get elevated visibility into your night and a clear, named responsibility to look out for you.

How to Assign a Wingman

Before activating Night Out Mode, you select your Wingman from your Circle. You can choose anyone in your Circle, and the choice can vary night to night, sometimes it is your flatmate who is staying in, sometimes it is a parent or a close friend who lives nearby. What matters is that this person knows they are your Wingman and what that means.

Once assigned, your Wingman receives a notification confirming their role. They can see your Night Out Mode status in real time, a live view of your activity, any alerts you send, and your general location through the evening.

"One named person, watching out for you. That personal accountability changes everything about how seriously people take the role."

What Your Wingman Can See

During your night out, your Wingman gets an elevated view compared to other Circle members. This includes:

  • Live status, Whether Night Out Mode is active, where you are in general terms, and any updates you share.
  • Alert notifications, Immediate alerts for I Feel Off taps, Safety Call uses, and SOS Beacon triggers.
  • Squad awareness, If you are using the squad monitoring feature, your Wingman can see the broader picture of your group's status.
  • Morning After Report, At the end of the night, they receive a complete summary of the evening.

Why One Person Beats the Whole Circle

Alerting your entire Circle to every Night Out event creates noise. Your parents, your work friends, your university flatmate, they all get pinged every time something happens, even minor things. That leads to alert fatigue and, paradoxically, less engagement when something serious occurs.

The Wingman model concentrates attention where it matters. Your Wingman knows that for this evening, they are your person. They are checking in, staying alert, and ready to respond. The focused responsibility that comes with being named creates genuine commitment, the kind that a group broadcast simply cannot replicate.

Your Circle is still in the loop for SOS-level events. But your Wingman is the dedicated first responder for the quieter moments that still matter. It is the difference between a safety net and a safety system, and Obserc is firmly in the latter camp.
